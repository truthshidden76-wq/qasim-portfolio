Keep Your Face the Same in Every AI Photo: The ChatGPT Custom Instructions Method

By Qasim Kayani · Qasim Media

AI photos have a familiar disappointment. The first picture looks like you. The second looks like a cousin. The background changed, the outfit changed, and somewhere in that change your face changed too. The eyes are a little different, the beard is a little neater, and the person in the frame is no longer quite you.

The thumbnail for this article promises a fix: "Keep Your Face The Same in Every AI Photo" and "SAME FACE EVERYWHERE". It shows five pictures of the same man in five places, from Paris to snowy mountains to a night street, with the same glasses, beard, and face in each one. This article explains what the thumbnail actually shows, why faces drift, and what to check before trusting any single setting to solve it.

What the Thumbnail Actually Shows

The right side of the thumbnail shows a phone screen in the ChatGPT app, open on Settings, then Personalization, then Custom Instructions. Under "What would you like ChatGPT to know about you?" there is a short instruction. It reads, word for word: "Preserve my face, skin tone, face shape, eyes, nose, beard and glasses."

That wording is the whole method as pictured. It names the features that must stay put, while the place, clothes, and weather are free to change. It draws a line between identity, which must not move, and scene, which may.

Be precise about what this proves. The thumbnail shows a setting and a sentence, not the setting working over many chats. Until that is confirmed, treat the wording as a starting point to test, not a guarantee.

Why Faces Drift

An image model does not have a perfect memory of your face. Every new picture is a fresh interpretation. If your description is vague, the model fills the gaps with guesses, and those guesses drift a little each time. A short prompt such as "a photo of me in Paris" gives it almost nothing to hold on to, so it invents the details it was not given.

That is why the guides found in a public search all make the same point: lock the description down and reuse it. Write down face shape, skin tone, eyes, eyebrows, nose, lips, jawline, hair, beard, and glasses. Use the same description every time, upload the same clear reference photo, and ask for one change at a time.

The Method as Shown

Taken at face value, the method has three parts. First, open ChatGPT Settings, go to Personalization, and open Custom Instructions. Second, enter the instruction under "What would you like ChatGPT to know about you?" using the exact wording shown. Third, generate photos as usual, changing only the scene, outfit, or background, while the instruction stays in place.

Most consistent-face advice adds a step the thumbnail does not show: upload a clear reference photo with each request. A sharp, well-lit photo, facing the camera, with no sunglasses and no heavy filter, gives the model something real to copy. A blurry selfie gives it blurry information, and it will guess the rest.

Repeat the keep-list in the prompt itself, especially in a new chat. A prompt that starts with "keep my face exactly the same", names the features to protect, and then asks for one single change is the pattern the published guides recommend. Changing background, clothes, lighting, pose, and expression all at once is how faces break.

What to Check Before You Trust It

Do not judge the method by one good picture. Generate several in different settings and compare them side by side with your original photo. Check face shape, apparent age, eyes, eyebrows, nose, mouth, jawline, skin details, and hairline, at normal size, because that is how other people will see the result. If a picture looks polished but the person is wrong, it has failed.

Start each new edit from your original photo, not from an AI copy of it. Editing a copy of a copy lets small errors compound. If a face has already drifted, do not keep editing the bad version. Go back to the original and start again, and save every good version before trying the next change.

No prompt wording can guarantee an identical face every time. If his test needed many retries, readers deserve to know, because they will face the same retries.

Use this method on your own face only. Do not generate realistic pictures of another real person without that person's clear agreement, and do not use AI edits for passport or identity photos. The test for social content is simple: does it still look like you at first glance, to someone who knows you?

A Short Checklist to Try

First, write your own full face description in plain language and read it back for errors. If it says your eyes are brown and they are hazel, fix it. Second, save the thumbnail's short instruction alongside your fuller description, so you can test both. Third, upload one clear reference photo and ask for a single change, such as a new background only. Fourth, compare the result with the original before trying a second change.

If the face holds across three or four settings, you have a method worth keeping. If it drifts, add detail, return to the original photo, and try again with one change at a time. The goal is fair: the same face everywhere. Reaching it takes a saved description, a good reference photo, and the patience to check each result.
