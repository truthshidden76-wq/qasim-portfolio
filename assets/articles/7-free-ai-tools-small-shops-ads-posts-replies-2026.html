7 Free AI Tools for Small Shops That Make Ads, Posts and Replies

A small shop owner is the designer, the copywriter, the social media manager and the customer service desk — usually before lunch. Hiring an agency is out of reach for most shops, and paying for five different subscriptions adds up fast. The good news is that the three jobs a shop actually needs — making ads, posting regularly, and replying to customers — can all be done with genuinely free tools in 2026.

Every tool below has a real free tier, not a 7-day trial that asks for a card. The free limits are stated honestly, because a free tool you outgrow in week two is not a bargain — it is a trap. Pick the ones that cover your weakest job first, and add the rest as you go.

What "Free" Actually Means Here

Free tiers are real products, but they are capped on purpose. A design tool limits templates, a scheduler limits how far ahead you can queue, and an AI tool limits how much it generates per day or month. None of the tools in this list stops working when you hit a limit — they slow down, watermark, or ask you to wait for the next reset. That is the deal, and it is a fair one for a shop posting a few times a week.

One rule before the list: never let a free tool touch a paying customer without a human check. AI writes a decent caption; it does not know your prices, your stock, or that a customer is angry. You are the quality control.

The 7 Best Free AI Tools for Small Shops

1. Canva (Free) — Best for Ad and Post Design

Canva is where most small-shop ads start, and for good reason. The free plan is permanent — $0 forever, no card — and it includes the full editor, 1.6 million-plus templates, 4.7 million-plus free photos, graphics and audio clips, 5GB of storage, and one Brand Kit limited to three colours. That covers a shop's daily needs: sale posters, product announcements, price lists, story graphics and a simple logo lockup.

Use it to build three or four reusable templates — one for new arrivals, one for offers, one for customer reviews — and stop designing from scratch every time. Change the photo, the price and one line of text, and the post is done.

Best for: ad creatives, product posts, sale posters, story graphics.

Honest limit: the AI allowance on free is small — a limited number of monthly AI uses — and the premium templates and stock (marked with a crown) are locked. Magic Resize, which reformats one design for every platform, is paid. Design in the size you actually post and you will rarely miss it.

2. Meta Business Suite (Free) — Best for Scheduling and Replies in One Place

Meta Business Suite is Meta's own free dashboard for a Facebook Page and a connected Instagram account, and it is the single most useful free tool on this list for a shop that sells on those two platforms. It is completely free — scheduling, the inbox and insights cost nothing; you only pay if you choose to run ads.

From one screen you can create and schedule posts, stories and reels, and answer every comment and direct message from Facebook Messenger, Instagram and WhatsApp Business in a single inbox. It also has automations: an instant reply when someone first messages you, an away message outside your hours, and answers to up to five frequently asked questions — so "What are your timings?" and "Do you deliver?" get answered while you are serving a customer in the shop.

Best for: scheduling posts, answering comments and DMs in one inbox, setting up automatic replies.

Honest limit: it only covers Meta platforms — Facebook, Instagram, Threads and WhatsApp. For TikTok, LinkedIn, Pinterest or X you still need another tool. Some extras, such as longer scheduling horizons and links in organic posts, now sit in Meta's paid Meta One plans.

3. ChatGPT (Free) — Best for Ad Copy, Captions and Replies

Every ad needs words, and a small shop writes more words than it realises: captions, offer announcements, product descriptions, replies to "price?" comments, and WhatsApp broadcasts. ChatGPT's free tier gives you GPT-4o access with daily limits — enough for a shop's daily writing if you batch it.

The trick is to feed it specifics. "Write a caption for a 20% off sale on handmade soaps, this weekend only, for Instagram, friendly tone, under 40 words" beats "write a caption" every time. Use it to draft five captions at once, three reply templates for common questions, and a short product description — then edit every line in your own voice before posting.

Best for: captions, ad copy, product descriptions, reply templates, offer announcements.

Honest limit: the free tier has daily usage limits, and the output is generic until you edit it. Never trust it with facts, prices or dates — it can invent details that look real, and a wrong price in an ad is your problem, not the AI's.

4. CapCut (Free) — Best for Video Ads and Reels

Short video sells more than a static image for most shops — a 15-second clip of a product being used, packed, or worn beats a photo every time. CapCut's free plan is the full editor: timeline, multi-track editing, keyframes, filters, transitions, text and music, with export up to 1080p.

A manual edit built only from free assets exports without a watermark. That is the detail most people get wrong about CapCut: the watermark appears only when a project uses a Pro-marked template, effect, filter or music track, or when a template appends its ending logo clip — which you can delete from the timeline before exporting.

Best for: product videos, reels, unboxing clips, before-and-after videos, video ads.

Honest limit: 4K and 60fps exports need Pro, and auto captions are capped on the free plan. For a shop posting reels and Shorts, 1080p at 30fps is the safe free ceiling — and it is what most viewers watch on their phones anyway.

5. Buffer (Free) — Best for Scheduling Across Channels

If your shop posts on Instagram, Facebook and TikTok, Buffer's free plan keeps you from opening three apps every morning. It is free forever: 3 connected channels, 10 scheduled posts per channel at a time, one user, 100 saved ideas, the AI Assistant, and 30 days of insights.

The 10-post number is a queue limit, not a monthly limit — the moment a scheduled post goes out, its slot frees up, so you can publish far more than 10 posts a month as long as you never queue more than 10 ahead. For a shop posting once a day on three channels, that is a full week planned in advance.

Best for: scheduling posts across Instagram, Facebook, TikTok and more; keeping a queue of ready posts.

Honest limit: three channels at once, and a lifetime limit of eight unique channel connections — if you disconnect and reconnect accounts often, you can burn through it. One user only, so a second person needs a paid plan.

6. Microsoft Designer (Free) — Best for AI Ad Images

Sometimes a shop needs an image that does not exist yet: a product shot on a festive background, a seasonal banner, a "new collection" hero image. Microsoft Designer is free with a Microsoft account and generates images with DALL-E 3. You get 15 fast boosts a day — after those run out, generation continues at a slower speed instead of stopping.

Type a plain description — "a handmade soap bar on a wooden table with green leaves, warm morning light" — and you get a usable image in seconds. It is the fastest way to make a seasonal ad visual without a photoshoot.

Best for: AI product images, seasonal banners, ad backgrounds, "new arrival" visuals.

Honest limit: 15 fast generations a day, then a slower queue that can be very slow at peak hours. Check the commercial-use terms before using AI-generated images in paid ads — free generations are aimed at personal and everyday use, and the rules for advertising use are worth reading first.

7. Predis.ai (Free) — Best for AI-Made Posts With Captions

Predis.ai is the closest thing to a one-click post maker on this list: type one line about your product or offer and it generates the caption, the hashtags and a matching visual at the same time. The free plan gives you 15 AI-generated posts a month and one brand, plus 10 competitor analysis runs.

For a shop that posts three or four times a week, 15 posts covers about a month of content. Use it for the posts you would otherwise skip — the Tuesday product highlight, the Friday offer reminder — and write the important ones yourself.

Best for: ready-made posts with caption and visual together, hashtag suggestions, quick offer posts.

Honest limit: 15 posts a month is tight, the visuals are template-based rather than fully original, and free-plan outputs carry a watermark. Paid plans start at around $29 to $32 a month, so treat free as a supplement, not your whole content calendar.

A 30-Minute Weekly Routine for a Small Shop

Here is how the seven tools combine into a routine that actually fits a shop owner's week:

  1. Design (10 minutes): In Canva, make three posts from your saved templates — a product highlight, an offer, and a customer review. If you need a fresh visual, generate one in Microsoft Designer first.
  2. Write (10 minutes): Ask ChatGPT for captions for all three posts and one reply template for the week's most common question. Edit every line — prices, timings and product names must be yours.
  3. Video (5 minutes, optional): If you shot a clip this week, cut it in CapCut, add captions, export at 1080p, and add it to the queue.
  4. Schedule (5 minutes): Queue the week in Buffer or Meta Business Suite. One post a day is plenty for most shops.
  5. Reply (10 minutes a day): Open the Meta Business Suite inbox once in the morning and once in the evening. Answer questions yourself — automations handle the greeting, you handle the sale.

Total: about 30 minutes of planning a week, plus 10 minutes of replies a day. The tools do the production; you do the selling.

Honest Pros and Limits

Where these free tools genuinely help a small shop:

  • Zero monthly cost — the whole stack costs nothing to start.
  • One person can run the shop's entire marketing in under an hour a week.
  • Scheduling means the shop posts even on your busiest days.
  • Automatic replies mean no customer waits hours for a first answer.

Where you must stay careful:

  • Free tiers are capped. Buffer's queue, Predis.ai's 15 posts, Canva's AI allowance and Designer's 15 daily boosts all run out — plan around them instead of discovering them mid-week.
  • Watermarks and templates look alike. Free Predis.ai outputs carry a watermark, and template-based visuals start to look like every other shop's. Your own photos and your own words are what make the feed yours.
  • AI does not know your business. Prices, stock, delivery areas and timings change — if the AI writes them, verify them. A wrong price in an ad costs more than the time you saved.
  • Replies need a human. Automations can greet and answer FAQs. Complaints, refunds and custom orders need you, fast — a slow human reply loses more sales than a missing post ever will.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I run my whole shop's marketing free?

For a small shop posting a few times a week on Facebook and Instagram, yes — this stack covers design, copy, video, scheduling and replies at zero cost. The moment you need more than 15 AI posts a month, more than three channels, or 4K video, one paid plan (usually the scheduler or the design tool) is the first thing worth buying.

Which tool should I start with if I only pick one?

Meta Business Suite, if you sell on Facebook and Instagram — it is free, it schedules, and its inbox is where sales actually happen. If your posts look weak, start with Canva instead. Design first, scheduling second.

Do free AI images work in paid ads?

They can, but read the terms first. Microsoft Designer's free generations are aimed at personal and everyday use, and advertising use has its own rules. Your own product photos are always the safest choice for a paid ad — AI images are best for backgrounds, banners and seasonal decoration.

How many posts a week can I schedule free?

Buffer's free plan holds 10 scheduled posts per channel at a time, which refills as posts publish — a full week of daily posts fits easily. Meta Business Suite schedules Facebook and Instagram posts without a published queue cap. Predis.ai's free plan only generates 15 posts a month, so use it for filler posts, not your whole calendar.

Will customers know I used AI?

They will if you post untouched AI text — generic captions and identical template visuals are easy to spot. After a real edit in your own voice, with your own photos and your own prices, the post sounds like your shop, because the decisions were yours.

The Bottom Line

A small shop does not need a marketing department — it needs a routine. Canva designs the ad, ChatGPT writes the words, CapCut cuts the video, Microsoft Designer makes the image, Predis.ai fills the gaps, and Buffer or Meta Business Suite puts it all on a schedule while the inbox answers first. Every one of those tools is free to start, and every limit on this list is one you can plan around.

Start with the two that fix your weakest job — usually design and replies — and add the rest one at a time. The tools are free. Your time is not, so spend it talking to customers, not fighting software.
