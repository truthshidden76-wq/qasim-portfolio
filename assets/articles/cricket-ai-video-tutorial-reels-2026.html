Create a Trending Cricket AI Video Tutorial for Reels

You have seen the clip: a dusty village pitch, a batter waggles the bat, taps it once, then smashes a stylish pull shot high into the sky. In slow motion the batter finishes on one leg, hides the face behind the bat and walks away smiling. It looks like a movie scene. It is made with one photo, one reference video and Google Flow, and this guide shows every step.

The trick is the reference video. Flow copies the exact camera position, the ball height, the bat swing and the slow-motion finish from it, and only replaces the person with your photo. Without the reference, Flow guesses the angle and the shot turns into a normal swing. With it, the clip matches the trend.

What You Need

  • One clear photo of yourself. Front-facing, good light, your real face, hair and clothes. This is the only identity reference. Flow will copy your face, skin tone and hairstyle from it.
  • A muted reference video of the shot. A short clip of the same pull shot, with the sound removed. A video with sound can cause an audio error in Flow, so use the muted file only.
  • Google Flow. A Google account, 18 or older. Flow gives 50 free credits a day.
  • CapCut or VN. For the trending song and the final export. The generated clip has no sound, so music is added after.

The Settings That Matter

Use these exact settings in Flow: Ingredients mode, 9:16 vertical ratio, 720p, 1x output, 10 seconds. Ingredients mode is what lets you attach the reference video and your photo together. The 9:16 ratio is the Reels format. Keep the output at 720p on the free plan.

Step by Step

  • 1. Get the muted reference video. Use a reference clip you have permission to use, and make sure it is the muted version. Do not use someone else’s video to publish as your own work without permission.
  • 2. Open Google Flow and start a new project. Go to labs.google/flow and sign in.
  • 3. Upload the reference video as @video1. This is the master. Flow will copy its camera, timing and movement frame by frame.
  • 4. Upload your photo as @image1. This is the only identity reference. Check the label points to your photo, not to the video.
  • 5. Paste the prompt below and generate. A 10-second clip on the free plan uses Flow credits, so expect to try two or three takes before one looks right.
  • 6. Add music in CapCut. Import the clip, add a trending song, trim the start and end, and export in 9:16 for Reels.

The Prompt (Copy and Paste)

Use @video1 for the reference video and @image1 for your photo. Paste this prompt into Flow:

“Use @video1 as the master reference. Recreate it exactly, frame by frame: the same camera position, camera angle, framing, camera distance and slow-motion timing, in one continuous shot. Copy every movement exactly: the batting stance, the bat waggles, the tap of the bat on the pitch, the backlift, the ball arriving at waist height, the pull shot and the swing, the ball flying up into the sky, the slow-motion one-leg finish, turning to the camera, hiding the face behind the bat, the smile, and walking away. Replace the person in @video1 with the person in @image1: same face, skin tone, hairstyle and clothes. Do not invent new scenes, do not cut to close-ups, do not change the camera angle. 9:16 vertical, 10 seconds.”

Why the Reference Video Matters

You can try the trend with only a prompt and your photo, but the result rarely matches. Flow often changes the camera angle, lets the ball bounce on the pitch, or turns the pull shot into a normal swing. The reference video fixes all three: the camera stays put, the ball arrives at the right height, and the swing follows the same path. That is why the guides for this trend all use the same method. A detailed walkthrough of the same reference-video method is at yetechnical.com/ai-cricket-viral-trend/.

Finish It Like a Reel

The generated clip is only half the reel. Add a trending cricket song in CapCut, keep the video under 10 seconds, and put the hook in the first second: the bat tap, not a title card. Export at 1080x1920 and post it as a Reel. The trend works for boys and girls alike, because the only thing that changes is the face in @image1.

Honest Limits

Flow gives 50 free credits a day and they do not roll over. Free exports are 720p and watermarked. The face can shift slightly between takes, and the bat or ball can glitch for a frame or two, so generate a few takes and keep the cleanest one. Only use a reference video you have permission to use, and label the result as AI-made so nobody mistakes it for a real match clip.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a bat, a ground or a camera?

No. You need one photo, one muted reference video and Google Flow. Nothing is filmed.

Why must the reference video be muted?

A video with sound can cause an audio or speech error in Flow. The muted file avoids that, and you add music afterwards in CapCut anyway.

Why does my shot look different from the trend?

Almost always the reference video was missing or the labels were swapped. Check that @video1 is the reference video and @image1 is your photo, and that the settings are Ingredients mode, 9:16, 720p, 10 seconds.

Can I use any viral clip as the reference?

Only one you have permission to use. If you plan to publish the result, make sure you follow the rules for the original video and any music you add.

The Bottom Line

One photo, one muted reference video, one prompt. That is the whole trend. Set Flow to Ingredients mode, 9:16, 720p and 10 seconds, paste the prompt, and let the reference video do the hard work. Add your song in CapCut and post it.
