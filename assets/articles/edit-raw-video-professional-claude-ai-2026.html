Edit Raw Video to Professional Video with Claude AI (FREE) — ChatGPT Master Prompt

Raw footage is not a bad video. It is an unfinished one. The difference between a raw clip and a professional video is a set of small, repeatable decisions — cut the dead space, fix the pacing, steady the exposure, add movement where the eye gets bored, and put sound where the silence feels empty. Claude AI can do the technical half of that work for free, if you ask it the right way.

This guide gives you the exact master prompt to paste into ChatGPT and Claude, the step-by-step Claude Code workflow, the Claude Desktop route, and the honest limits — including what Claude cannot do, and the CapCut finishing pass that turns a clean edit into a professional one. Everything here was tested on a real raw video, and the limits are stated exactly as they were found.

What Claude Can Actually Do to Your Video (Honest Answer)

Claude Code edits video by running ffmpeg commands. That is its real power and its real limit. It can read your video file, look at its frames, and run precise technical operations on it. It cannot watch your video the way a human editor watches it, and it cannot replicate the style of a reference video just by looking at it.

What it does well, for free: convert frame rate (for example 24 fps to 30 fps), cut dead space and long pauses, trim clips to a rhythm, and apply basic colour and exposure normalisation across the whole clip. What it cannot do: frame-by-frame compositing or masking, sophisticated motion graphics or kinetic typography, high-end audio mixing, granular colour grading, or copying a reference video's editing style. If someone promises you all of that from Claude alone, they have not tested it.

That is not a reason to skip Claude. It is a reason to use it for the job it is good at — the technical first pass — and finish the video in CapCut, where the zoom, the sound effects and the captions live.

The ChatGPT Master Prompt (Copy and Paste)

Paste this into ChatGPT first, fill in your video's details, then paste the result into Claude Code. It is written so Claude can only promise what ffmpeg can actually deliver.

You are my video editing assistant. I have a raw video file and I want a clean, professional-looking cut using only ffmpeg commands.

VIDEO DETAILS
- File: [your video file name, for example raw-video.mp4]
- Length: [for example 38 seconds]
- Size: [for example 720x1280 vertical]
- Frame rate: [for example 24 fps]
- Goal: a clean, watchable short video for Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts.

DO THIS, IN ORDER
1. Run ffprobe on the video and report the real duration, resolution, frame rate and audio streams.
2. Find the dead space: long silences, shaky starts, and any section where nothing changes for more than 2 seconds. List each cut with start and end timestamps.
3. Cut the dead space and rejoin the remaining clips with smooth, hard cuts. Keep the total length tight — remove at least 15 percent of the runtime.
4. Convert the frame rate to 30 fps for smooth playback on Reels and Shorts.
5. Normalise exposure and colour across the whole video with one consistent ffmpeg filter, so no clip looks brighter or duller than the next.
6. Add a slow zoom-in (zoompan) on the 2 or 3 most important moments, so the frame has movement where the viewer would otherwise get bored.
7. Keep the original audio untouched. Do NOT add music or sound effects yourself. Instead, list the exact timestamps where I should add a whoosh, a pop or background music in CapCut.
8. Export as one MP4 file, same 720x1280 size, 30 fps, high quality, named Edited_Raw_Video.mp4.

RULES
- Use only ffmpeg commands. Do not claim any effect you cannot run with ffmpeg.
- Show me every command before you run it, and report the exact output file, size and duration when you finish.
- If a step is not possible with ffmpeg, say so plainly instead of pretending.

Why this prompt works: it tells Claude the exact operations, the exact export, and the exact rule that stops it from inventing effects. The line about sound effects matters most — Claude cannot mix audio, so the prompt makes it mark the timestamps and lets you add the sound in CapCut, where it belongs.

Step by Step: The Claude Code Route (Free)

This is the route that costs nothing and runs on your own computer.

  1. Put everything in one folder: your raw video file and the master prompt. Keep the folder on a drive with free space — video exports need room.
  2. Open Claude Code in that folder: open a terminal in the folder and start Claude Code. Check ffmpeg is installed first — Claude Code uses it for every video operation.
  3. Paste the master prompt: fill in your video's real details first. Wrong details give you a wrong edit.
  4. Let it probe first: Claude runs ffprobe and reports the real duration, resolution and frame rate. Check those numbers against your video before it cuts anything.
  5. Review the cut list: Claude lists every cut with timestamps. If it wants to cut a moment you need, say so before it renders.
  6. Check the export: open Edited_Raw_Video.mp4 and watch it end to end. Check the length, the pacing and whether any clip looks brighter or duller than the rest.

Expect the first pass to look cleaner but not yet professional. That is normal. The professional look comes from the CapCut pass below.

Step by Step: The Claude Desktop Route

Claude Desktop (Cowork) edits with prompts instead of commands, and it can work on the video more directly. Two things to know before you start: it needs free disk space to work — if the app says there is not enough space, free space on the drive first and try again — and when the model is busy you may see a capacity error. That error means the server is busy, not that your computer is broken. Wait a few minutes and press try again.

The same master prompt works here. Paste it, attach the video, and let Claude do the technical pass. Then take the result into CapCut for the finishing steps.

The CapCut Finishing Pass (Where the Pro Look Comes From)

Claude gives you a clean cut. CapCut gives you the professional feel. Do these five things, in order:

  1. Zoom in and out: add a slow zoom-in on the key sentence and a zoom-out on the transition. Small movement keeps the eye from drifting.
  2. Sound effects: add a whoosh on every cut that changes the scene and a pop on the on-screen text. Keep the effects quieter than the voice.
  3. Captions: add English captions for the whole video. Most viewers watch with the sound off first, and captions decide whether they stay.
  4. Background music: one low track under the voice, not over it. If the music is louder than the voice, the video is unwatchable.
  5. Colour check: watch the video on your phone, not only on the computer. If a clip looks dull on the phone, lift its brightness slightly in CapCut.

Five steps, about fifteen minutes. This is the part Claude cannot do for you, and it is the part viewers actually notice.

Honest Pros and Limits

Where Claude genuinely helps:

  • It finds dead space and cuts it precisely, with timestamps you can check.
  • Frame rate conversion and colour normalisation are exact, repeatable operations.
  • The whole technical pass is free, and it runs on your own files.
  • It never gets tired of re-cutting the same clip five times.

Where you must stay careful:

  • It cannot copy a reference style. Showing Claude a reference video does not make it edit like that video. It can run ffmpeg commands; it cannot see style the way you do.
  • It cannot do motion graphics, masking or kinetic typography. Those need an editor, not a command line.
  • It cannot mix audio. Music and sound effects are your job in CapCut.
  • A clean cut is not a finished video. If the first export looks almost the same as the raw video, that is Claude doing its job — the visible change comes from the finishing pass.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is editing with Claude really free?

Yes. Claude Code runs ffmpeg on your own computer, and the editing itself costs nothing. You only need the video file, the prompt, and enough free disk space for the export.

Why did my first Claude edit look almost the same as the raw video?

Because Claude's job is the technical pass — cuts, frame rate, colour normalisation. Those changes are real but subtle. The visible transformation comes from zoom, sound effects and captions in CapCut. If you skip the finishing pass, the video stays plain.

Can Claude add B-roll or a reference video's style?

No. Claude Code cannot composite B-roll over your footage or replicate a reference video's editing style. Add B-roll yourself in CapCut, where you can see exactly where it lands.

Do I still need CapCut if I use Claude?

Yes. Claude does the technical cut, CapCut does the professional finish. The two together are the full workflow — neither one replaces the other.

What if Claude Desktop says there is not enough disk space?

Free space on the drive first, then try again. Video work needs room for the export. If the app also shows a capacity or rate error, the server is busy — wait a few minutes and press try again.

The Bottom Line

Raw to professional is two jobs, not one. Claude does the technical half for free — precise cuts, frame rate, clean colour — and CapCut does the visible half — zoom, sound, captions, music. Use the master prompt above, check every cut before it renders, and finish every video with the fifteen-minute CapCut pass. That is the whole workflow, and it works because each tool does the job it is actually good at.
