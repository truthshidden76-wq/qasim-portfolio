AI Tools That Plan a Full Week of Content in One Sitting

Most creators do not fail because they run out of ideas. They fail because every day starts with the same question — "what do I post today?" — and by the time they answer it, the day is gone. The fix is not more motivation. It is one sitting, once a week, where the whole week gets planned, written, designed and scheduled before Monday even starts.

This guide shows you how to do that with seven genuinely free tools. One plans the week, one moves ideas through it, one writes the words, one designs the posts, one schedules them, one blocks the time, and one tells you what actually worked. Total cost: zero. Total time: about 90 minutes, once a week.

What One Sitting Actually Looks Like

A planning sitting has four jobs, in order: decide what goes out each day, write the words, design the visuals, and schedule everything. Most people mix the jobs together — they write Monday's caption, then scroll for inspiration, then design Tuesday's image, then forget what Wednesday was supposed to be. The sitting works because the jobs stay separate and each tool does exactly one of them.

The order matters. Plan first, while your head is clear. Write second, while the plan is fresh. Design third, in one batch, so you are not opening the design app seven times. Schedule last, when everything is ready to go.

The 7 Free Tools That Plan Your Week

1. Notion (Free) — Best for the Content Calendar

Notion is where the week lives. The free plan is completely free for one person — unlimited pages and blocks, databases, calendars and templates — so you can build a simple content calendar: one database, one row per day, with columns for the idea, the format, the caption status and the posting time.

Make a template for the week: Monday is a tip, Tuesday is a tool demo, Wednesday is a story, Thursday is a question, Friday is an offer, Saturday is behind the scenes, Sunday is rest or a repost. When the structure repeats every week, you stop deciding what to post and start deciding only how to say it.

Best for: the weekly content calendar, idea bank, keeping every caption and link in one place.

Honest limit: free uploads are capped at 5MB per file, page history only goes back 7 days, and you get 10 external guests. The real catch: the moment a second member joins your workspace, the free plan caps the whole workspace at 1,000 blocks. For one person planning alone, none of that matters. Notion AI is only a limited trial on free — the calendar itself is the point, not the AI.

2. Trello (Free) — Best for Moving Ideas Through the Week

Notion holds the plan; Trello moves it. Trello's free plan gives you 10 boards per workspace, 10 collaborators, unlimited cards, unlimited Power-Ups per board, and 250 automation runs a month — more than enough for one person's content week.

Make one board called "This Week" with four lists: Ideas, Writing, Designing, Scheduled. Every idea starts as a card in Ideas. When you write its caption, drag it to Writing. When the visual is done, drag it to Designing. When it is scheduled, drag it to Scheduled. On Sunday night the board is empty except for Scheduled — and next week's Ideas list is already half full from things you noticed during the week.

Best for: a visual weekly board, tracking each post from idea to scheduled, never losing a half-finished caption.

Honest limit: 10 boards per workspace and 10 collaborators, files capped at 10MB each, and 250 automation runs a month. One weekly board uses almost none of that. If you run out of boards, archive last month's board instead of opening a new workspace.

3. ChatGPT (Free) — Best for the Week's Ideas and Captions

The slowest part of planning is writing seven captions from a blank page. ChatGPT's free tier gives you GPT-4o access with daily limits — enough to draft a whole week's captions in one sitting if you batch them.

Do not ask for one caption at a time. Paste your week's plan and ask for all seven at once: "Here are my 7 post ideas for the week. Write one caption each, under 40 words, friendly tone, one clear call to action per caption." Then edit every caption yourself — fix the product names, the prices, the timings, and anything that sounds like a robot wrote it. The AI drafts; you decide.

Best for: generating the week's post ideas, drafting all seven captions at once, rewriting a weak caption three different ways.

Honest limit: the free tier has daily usage limits, so do the writing in one batch rather than coming back all day. And never trust it with facts — it can invent prices, dates and claims with total confidence. Every number in a caption is yours to check.

4. Buffer (Free) — Best for Scheduling the Week's Posts

Buffer is where the finished week goes to wait. The free plan is free forever: 3 connected channels, 10 scheduled posts per channel at a time, one user, 100 saved ideas, the AI Assistant, and 30 days of insights.

Seven daily posts fit easily inside the 10-post queue on each channel, and the queue refills the moment a post publishes — so Monday's post going out frees a slot for next Monday before you even sit down to plan. Connect Instagram, Facebook and TikTok, paste in the week's captions and visuals, pick the times, and the week runs itself.

Best for: scheduling the whole week's posts in one go, across three channels, with a queue that refills itself.

Honest limit: three channels at once, and a lifetime limit of eight unique channel connections — do not disconnect and reconnect accounts casually. One user only. If your week needs a fourth channel, schedule that one natively in its own app.

5. Google Calendar (Free) — Best for Time-Blocking the Sitting

A planning sitting only happens if it is on the calendar. Google Calendar is free with any Google account, and its only job here is protecting the time: one recurring 90-minute block every week, same day, same time, called "Content Planning — do not move".

Put the posting times in the same calendar. If your audience is most active at 9 in the morning and 6 in the evening, those times become repeating events, so Buffer's schedule matches the calendar and you never have to remember when things go out. The calendar is also where you see the truth: if the block keeps getting moved, the system is not failing — the time is not protected.

Best for: a fixed weekly planning block, posting times you can see, a record of what actually went out.

Honest limit: it is a calendar, not a content tool — it will not write, design or schedule anything. Its value is entirely in the habit. Skip the block twice and the week falls back to daily scrambling.

6. Adobe Express (Free) — Best for Designing All Seven Posts Fast

Designing seven posts one by one is how a planning sitting dies. Adobe Express's free plan — $0, no card — includes 100,000-plus templates, 4,000-plus fonts, 5GB of storage, and free daily AI generations for images, text effects and templates. Batch the design work: open your three saved templates, swap the text and photo for each day, export all seven, done.

Pick one template per format and reuse it all week — one for tips, one for quotes, one for offers. Your followers recognise the format before they read the words, and you stop redesigning from zero every single day.

Best for: designing a week's posts in one batch, reusable templates, quick AI-generated visuals.

Honest limit: Adobe does not publish the exact number of free daily AI generations — the allowance is small and it refreshes the next day, so save AI generations for the posts that need them and use templates for the rest. Premium templates, Brand Kits and one-click resize are paid; Premium is $9.99 a month with 250 generative credits.

7. Metricool (Free) — Best for Checking What Worked

Planning is only half the system — the other half is learning from last week before you plan the next one. Metricool's free plan is free forever for one brand: 20 scheduled posts a month, 30 days of analytics history, 5 competitor profiles, and 5 AI assistant credits a month.

Spend the first 10 minutes of your sitting in Metricool: which of last week's posts got the most saves, which time of day worked, which format flopped. Then plan this week around the answers instead of guessing again. Twenty scheduled posts a month covers about five posts a week, so if you post daily, use Metricool for analytics and let Buffer do the scheduling.

Best for: seeing best posting times, comparing last week's posts, tracking five competitors.

Honest limit: 20 scheduled posts a month is not enough for daily posting, and the free plan cannot connect LinkedIn or X at all. Use it for Instagram, Facebook, TikTok and Pinterest — and for the analytics, which is the part that actually improves next week.

The 90-Minute Weekly Sitting, Step by Step

Here is the whole sitting, with the clock running:

  1. Review last week (10 minutes): Open Metricool. Note the best post, the worst post and the best time of day. Write one line in Notion: "Do more of X, less of Y."
  2. Plan the week (15 minutes): In Notion, fill the seven rows — one idea per day, one format per day. Move the best ideas from the idea bank. If a day is blank after five minutes, repeat a format that worked last week.
  3. Write the captions (20 minutes): Paste the plan into ChatGPT, get seven draft captions, then edit all seven yourself. Prices, names and timings come from you, not the AI.
  4. Design the visuals (30 minutes): In Adobe Express, run your three templates through the week's seven posts. Swap text and photos, export everything into one folder.
  5. Schedule everything (10 minutes): In Buffer, attach each visual and caption to its day and time. Check the queue: seven posts, seven days, nothing missing.
  6. Move the board (5 minutes): In Trello, drag every card to Scheduled. Next week's Ideas list gets anything you noticed but did not use.

Ninety minutes, once a week. The rest of the week you just reply to comments.

Honest Pros and Limits

Where this system genuinely works:

  • One sitting replaces seven daily "what do I post" decisions.
  • Batching the writing and design cuts the per-post time by more than half.
  • Scheduling means the week posts itself, even on your busiest days.
  • Reviewing last week before planning the next one compounds — the calendar gets better every week.

Where you must stay careful:

  • Free tiers are capped. Buffer holds 10 posts per channel, Metricool schedules 20 a month, Notion caps files at 5MB, and Adobe Express does not publish its daily AI allowance. Plan inside the caps instead of hitting them on Thursday.
  • A plan is not a post. Seven scheduled posts still need you to reply to comments — scheduling without replies is a broadcast, not a conversation.
  • The sitting must stay fixed. Move it twice and the system collapses back into daily scrambling. Protect the 90 minutes like a client meeting.
  • AI captions need your edit. Seven untouched AI captions in a row are obvious to anyone who reads your feed twice. Your voice is the product; the AI is the first draft.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does the weekly sitting really take?

About 90 minutes once you have done it twice. The first sitting takes longer — closer to two hours — because you are building the Notion template and the Trello board. From the third week on, the templates do most of the work and the sitting drops under 90 minutes.

Can I plan a whole week free?

Yes. Notion, Trello, ChatGPT, Buffer, Google Calendar, Adobe Express and Metricool all have genuinely free tiers that cover a week of daily posts. The only hard ceiling is Metricool's 20 scheduled posts a month — use Buffer for scheduling and Metricool for analytics, and the free stack covers a full week.

What if I post more than once a day?

Buffer's free queue holds 10 posts per channel, which refills as posts publish, so two posts a day still fits if you top the queue up mid-week. If you post three times a day on several channels, you will outgrow the free queue in days — that is the honest point to pay for a scheduler, not a reason to skip planning.

Do I need all seven tools?

No. The minimum working system is three: Notion for the plan, ChatGPT for the words, Buffer for the schedule. Add Trello when you start losing ideas, Adobe Express when design becomes the bottleneck, Google Calendar when the sitting keeps slipping, and Metricool when you want to know what actually worked.

What if I miss a week?

Plan the next week anyway. A missed week is not a failed system — it is one blank row in the calendar. The worst response to a missed week is abandoning the sitting entirely; the best is a shorter one, even 30 minutes, that gets three posts scheduled instead of seven.

The Bottom Line

Content planning is not a talent — it is a calendar appointment. Notion holds the week, Trello moves the cards, ChatGPT drafts the words, Adobe Express designs the posts, Buffer schedules them, Google Calendar protects the time, and Metricool tells you what to change next week. Seven free tools, one 90-minute sitting, and the question "what do I post today?" disappears for good.

Book the sitting for the same time every week, protect it like a client meeting, and let the tools do the production while you do the part only you can do: talking to the people who show up.
