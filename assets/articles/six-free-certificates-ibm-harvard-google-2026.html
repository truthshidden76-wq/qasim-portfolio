6 Free Certificates From IBM, Harvard CS50 and Google Skillshop: What to Check Before You Start

By Qasim Kayani · Qasim Media

Instagram · Services & Pricing · WhatsApp

Six free certificates. Better skills, better jobs. The thumbnail promises a clean chain: learn, certify, interview, dream job. It shows six panels, three named providers and three subject areas, a checklist that reads skills, certificates, better jobs, and a job interview sheet that adds confidence to the list. It is an appealing promise, and it is exactly the kind of promise that needs checking before anyone spends weeks on it.

In online learning, "free" usually means one of three things. Sometimes the course is free and the certificate is free. Sometimes the course is free and the certificate is paid. Sometimes the course is free to audit, which means you can watch and read, but graded work and the certificate sit behind payment. All three get marketed as "free courses with certificates". Only the first matches what most learners think they are signing up for. This article goes through the six panels one by one, says what was verified about each route, and gives a short checklist to run before you start any of them.

The Six Panels, and What Each One Means

IBM Cognitive Class and Data Science Fundamentals

IBM Cognitive Class, formerly Big Data University, is described in multiple guides as a free online learning platform from IBM covering data science, artificial intelligence, machine learning and related technologies. Courses are described as self-paced, and completion is described as earning digital badges and certificates without a separate certificate fee. One guide puts it plainly: unlike many platforms that charge for certificates, Cognitive Class provides digital badges and certificates on completion. Another notes that learners can receive both a badge and a completion certificate, and that badges can be shown on a LinkedIn profile.

That comes with a distinction the platform itself now draws. Its own learn-more page describes free courses as building foundational skills, while a premium subscription adds structured learning paths, hands-on projects and official IBM Professional Certificates. It says free users receive a badge of completion for every course finished, while premium subscribers can earn official IBM Professional Certificates for designated programmes. The free award and the professional certificate may not be the same credential, so check the exact course page to see which award the course you choose actually gives. A badge, a completion certificate and a professional certificate are different claims, and employers read them differently.

Data Science Fundamentals is described as an IBM Cognitive Class learning path covering statistical analysis, data visualisation and exploratory data analysis, with courses including data science methodology, data visualisation with Python and statistics. Check the live learning path page, because a path can contain several courses, and you need to know whether the award comes from finishing the whole path or each course separately. Start with one course that matches the skill you need next, finish it completely, and save whatever award it issues with your name spelled as you want an employer to see it.

Harvard CS50

Harvard CS50 is the best documented route on the list, and the distinction it draws explains most "free Harvard certificate" confusion. CS50 is Harvard's introduction to computer science. Multiple guides agree on the same structure: the course is free to study, and there are two different certificates. The first is the CS50 Certificate itself, issued by CS50, described as free and earned by scoring at least 70 percent on every problem set, lab and final project, claimed through cs50.harvard.edu. The second is a verified certificate through edX, which is paid. Prices reported for the verified certificate differ between guides, so this article gives no price. Check the live enrolment page before paying anything, and be clear which certificate you are pursuing before you start.

Google Skillshop and Digital Marketing

Google Skillshop is Google's free online training hub for its advertising and marketing products, including Google Ads, Google Analytics and YouTube. Guides that checked Google's own help pages describe the same pattern: creating an account, taking the courses and sitting the assessments are free, you sign in with a Google account, and passing an assessment earns a certification for that product area. One guide reports an 80 percent pass mark for Google Ads certifications, a 75-minute limit, a one-year validity, and renewal by retaking the assessment. Those details come from a secondary guide, not a live check of Skillshop for this article, so confirm them on the assessment page before you sit it.

A Skillshop certification is tied to a specific product. A Google Ads Search certification does not claim you are a marketer in general. It claims you passed an assessment on that product area. Pick the certification that matches the work you want. One finished, relevant certification beats four unrelated ones on a CV. Be careful with lookalike sites: Skillshop is run by Google, and any site asking you to pay for a Skillshop certificate or exam answers should be avoided.

Web Development Fundamentals, and How to Check Any Claim

Web Development Fundamentals is the panel that could not be verified at all. The thumbnail names no provider, no platform and no course page for it, and research for this article found no source tying that exact title to a specific free certificate route. Do not present it as a named provider's certificate until Qasim confirms which course he means, on which platform, and what it awards. There are many free web development courses, and many are excellent, but "many" is not a plan. A plan names one course, one page and one award.

Certificates do not hire you alone. A certificate says you completed a course. A project says you applied it. Pair each certificate with one piece of work: a small analysis, a campaign plan for a real small business, or a finished page that loads fast on a phone. Finish one course, build one thing with it, and only then start the next. Trying to run all six at once is how learners end up with six enrolments and no certificates.
