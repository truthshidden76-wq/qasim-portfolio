5 ChatGPT Photo Commands: Sunset Sky, Ghost Effect, Fire Border, Light Trails and Gangster Style

By Qasim Kayani · Qasim Media

The thumbnail promises something every creator wants: five photo effects, five short commands, no long prompt to write. It shows five portraits of the same man, each transformed differently. Behind one is a burning orange sunset over water. In the next, faded copies of his profile hover on either side like ghosts. The middle portrait is framed in flames. The fourth is wrapped in golden light trails. The fifth sits in a dark room, in a leather chair, under a warm lamp, in what the label calls gangster style. The labels read like commands: /sunset sky, /ghost effect, /fire border, /light trails, /gangster style. This article explains what those labels really are, how to use them for the best chance of a good result, and what to check before any edited portrait goes public.

The most important fact comes first, because the thumbnail does not state it. These are not official ChatGPT features. OpenAI does not list slash commands for photo effects, and typing a slash word does not activate a hidden built-in effect. Independent guides that collect ChatGPT image commands say this plainly: the slash labels are shorthand prompts, short names for visual ideas that ChatGPT reads as ordinary words. It does not make the effect automatic, and it does not guarantee the same result twice. You are giving a short, clear instruction in a reusable form, then checking what was actually produced.

How to Use a Slash Label

A bare label alone is the weakest form of the instruction. It names an effect and nothing else: not which photo to change, not what to keep, and not how far to go. A stronger instruction says all three. Upload the photo, then write a full sentence around the label. For example: Edit this image with /sunset sky. Keep my face, outfit, and pose unchanged, and replace only the background with a sunset sky. That wording names the effect, names the photo as the thing to edit, and draws a boundary around what must not change.

That boundary matters because an AI image edit is not a filter applied to your original pixels. ChatGPT generates a new image guided by your photo and your words. The face, hair, glasses, and outfit can all shift, sometimes slightly and sometimes badly. Guides that test these shortcuts agree: results vary with the photo, and the more specific your instruction about what to preserve, the better your chances. Always compare the edited portrait with the original, side by side, before you use it. Check the face first, then the hands, then any text or small detail. If the likeness has drifted, do not post the edit as a photo of yourself. Call it an AI edit, or discard it and try again with a tighter instruction.

The Five Effects

Sunset sky, labelled /sunset sky, shows a portrait against a vivid orange and red sunset, sun low over water, sky filling the background. Person, suit, and pose stay the same; only the setting changes. Ask for it that way: replace the background with a sunset sky, keep the person unchanged, and keep the light on the face believable against the new sky. If the face stays lit as though indoors while the sky burns orange, the edit looks pasted together.

Ghost effect, labelled /ghost effect, shows the portrait in the centre, with faded, semi-transparent copies of his profile on both sides, in a cool blue-grey palette. Ask for faded duplicate profiles on either side, with the central portrait sharp and the face recognisable in the centre. Watch for the common failure: a duplicate sharp enough to compete with the original, or a ghost copy whose features do not match. The copies should read as echoes. If they read as two extra people, the effect has gone too far, and a follow-up asking for softer, more transparent copies further out is the fix to try.

Fire border, labelled /fire border, shows the portrait framed by a bright ring of flames, subject untouched inside. It is the most dramatic of the five, and the easiest to overdo. Ask for a flame border around the edges, with person, face, and clothing unchanged, and no fire touching the subject. Check that flames sit at the edges, not across the face or hair, and that no part of the portrait has been scorched or redrawn to fit the frame. A fire border should decorate the portrait. If it starts to consume it, the edit has failed, however impressive the flames look alone.

Light trails, labelled /light trails, shows golden and red streaks swirling around the portrait, movement in the background, subject still and sharp in the centre. Ask for flowing light trails around the subject, kept in the background and away from the face, with the person unchanged. The failure to watch for is light crossing the face, washing out features, or turning the portrait into a pattern where the person is hard to find. Light trails work when they contrast with stillness: a calm, sharp subject and movement everywhere else.

Gangster style, labelled /gangster style, shows the subject in a dark, wood-panelled room, seated in a leather chair, warm table lamp behind him, serious and formal. Ask for it as a scene change, not a character change: place the same person in a dark study with a leather chair and warm lamplight, keep face, outfit, and pose, and keep the mood formal and cinematic. Be careful with the label. Gangster style here means a film look, a mood and a setting, not a claim about any real person or activity. Present the result as a styled portrait, nothing more.

Combining, Privacy, and Honest Presentation

The temptation with five short labels is to stack them: sunset, light trails, and fire border in one message, for one spectacular portrait. Resist it. Each effect pulls the image in a different direction, and a stacked request gives ChatGPT no clear priority when they conflict. A sunset sky and a dark study cannot both be the background. Use one effect at a time. Get one right and keep it. Then try another on its own, compare the two finished portraits, and choose the stronger one, rather than forcing both into a single crowded image.

Only edit photos you own or have clear permission to edit, and remember that uploading a photo sends it to the tool, so keep the original safe on your own device. Finally, present an AI-edited portrait honestly. A short caption, AI edit, or edited with ChatGPT, costs nothing and protects your credibility.
