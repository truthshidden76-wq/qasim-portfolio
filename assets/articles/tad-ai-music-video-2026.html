Tad AI Music Video: Turn Photos and a Song Into a Music Video in 9:16

Six numbered steps run down the screen: upload reference photos, upload music, describe the video scene, choose an aspect ratio, choose a resolution, and generate. On the left, the finished idea plays out as a film strip: the same person in different locations at sunset, a city skyline, a mountain road, a video player at 28 seconds. That recipe is clear, and it is also untested in this draft. This article explains the flow exactly as the thumbnail shows it, explains what Tad AI is reported to be, and marks every detail that must be confirmed by Qasim's own test before the recipe is presented as fact.

What Tad AI Is Reported To Be

Tad AI is described in third party listings checked for this article as an AI music production platform. Its core work is generating songs, with lyrics, melody, and vocals, from a text prompt, alongside rap generation, cover versions, and music video generation for the tracks created. It is also described as bundling audio tools such as text to speech, vocal removal, and stem separation. The platform is described as freemium, running on a credit system, with a free allowance and paid plans that add credits and commercial rights.

None of that listing material is Tad AI's own site, and this article does not treat it as official. It is included only to give a reader context: Tad AI is a music platform first, and the music video feature sits inside that platform, rather than being a standalone video editor.

The Six Steps, As The Thumbnail Shows Them

Step one is Upload Reference Photos. The thumbnail shows three photos of the same person in different settings, plus a plus button for more. The point of reference photos is consistency: the generated video should show the same person across its scenes, rather than a different face in every shot. The thumbnail does not say how many photos are required or how clear they must be. Clear, front facing photos of one person will give the tool the most to work with.

Step two is Upload Music, marked in the thumbnail as 10 to 30 seconds. The example file is named travel-music.mp3 and runs 28 seconds. That example sits neatly inside the stated range, which is convenient for a thumbnail and suspiciously neat for a product limit. It may be the real limit. It may be an illustration of a short clip. This article does not assert it as a verified product rule.

Step three is Describe Your Video Scene. The example description reads: "A cinematic travel music video of me in different locations, sunset vibes, cinematic shots, smooth transitions..." That description does three jobs at once. It sets the genre, travel. It sets the mood, sunset and cinematic. And it sets the movement, different locations and smooth transitions. A description written that way gives the generator concrete things to show, rather than a vague request for a nice video. A reader writing their own description should name the locations, the time of day, the mood, and the kind of shots they want, in that order, and keep it to one clear paragraph.

Steps four and five are the technical choices: Aspect Ratio 9:16 and Resolution 720p. Nine by sixteen is the vertical shape for phones, for TikTok, Reels, and Shorts. Seven hundred and twenty p is a mid level resolution, watchable on a phone, lighter to generate and share than full high definition. The thumbnail shows those as selected options in dropdowns, which implies other options exist, but it does not show what they are.

Step six is Generate, marked 46 Credits, with a red button reading 46 Credits. That is the price shown in the design. It is not a verified current price. Third party pricing checked here gives different credit figures for different Tad AI tiers and does not confirm a 46 credit music video cost. Until that is confirmed, 46 credits should be read as "the cost shown in the thumbnail," not as "the cost today."

What To Check In The Finished Video

A music video made this way should be judged on four things before posting. First, identity: does the person in the video look like the person in the reference photos, in every scene, or does the face drift? Second, sync: do the scene changes feel connected to the music, or do they float separately from it? Third, locations: does the video actually show different locations, as the description asked, or does it repeat one background? Fourth, polish: are there visual glitches, warped faces, or artefacts that break the illusion on a phone screen?

If any of those four fails, change one input and try again, rather than posting a flawed video and hoping viewers will not notice. Change the description first, because it costs nothing to rewrite. Then change the reference photos, if identity is the problem. Then change the song clip, if sync is the problem. Each retry may cost credits, which is why the credit question above matters so much. A workflow that needs three tries at 46 credits each is a different proposition from one that works first time.

Rights are the fifth check, and the one most easily skipped. Third party listings checked here say free credits on Tad AI are non commercial and that paid plans add commercial rights, but that is a general statement about the platform, not a verified term for AI MV videos.

Who This Suits

This flow suits a creator who wants a personal music video without a shoot: a travel video from their own photos, a song they own or have the right to use, and a vertical format for social platforms. It is a weaker fit for anyone who needs exact control over every shot, lip sync to specific lyrics, or a long form video, because the flow as shown is built for a short clip shaped by a description, not for a frame by frame edit.

Try it once, with a short clip you own, clear reference photos, and a specific description, and judge the result on a phone before you build a series around it.
