3 Free SEO Tools That Turn Claude Code Into an SEO Expert

Paid SEO Tools Are Expensive. These Three Free Tools Change That

Every serious SEO platform seems to come with a serious monthly price. Tools like Ahrefs and Semrush are powerful, but for a creator, freelancer, or small business owner, their subscriptions can cost more than the content they are meant to improve. Many people therefore skip SEO entirely, or guess at keywords and hope for the best.

There is a cheaper way to work, and it starts with a tool many creators already know: Claude Code. On its own, Claude Code is an AI coding assistant that can read, write, and reason about your project. Connected to the right SEO tools, it becomes something more useful: an assistant that can look at your real search data, research keywords, check your rankings, and even test how your content appears in AI search. This guide covers three free tools that make that possible: Search Console MCP, Open SEO, and Geo Optimizer. Each one is explained in the same order: what it does, who it suits, and how it fits a Claude Code SEO workflow.

What Is Claude Code, and Why Pair It With SEO Tools?

Claude Code is an AI assistant that works directly with your files and projects. Instead of chatting in a browser tab and copying answers back and forth, you ask it questions and give it tasks inside your working environment, and it acts on your actual content.

The connection that makes the SEO workflow possible is MCP, the Model Context Protocol. MCP is an open protocol that lets an AI assistant connect to external tools and data sources. A Search Console MCP connection, for example, lets Claude Code read your Search Console data instead of guessing about it. That is the whole idea behind this guide: stop asking an AI to guess about your SEO, and start letting it work with your real data, your real rankings, and your real content.

Tool 1: Search Console MCP

What it does. Search Console MCP connects Claude Code to your Google Search Console data. Instead of opening dashboards and exporting spreadsheets by hand, you can ask about your own SEO data directly: which queries bring people to your site, which pages get impressions but few clicks, and where keyword opportunities are hiding. It turns your Search Console data into answers you can act on, and its job is to help you find the keywords and pages that can rank higher on Google.

Who it suits. This tool suits anyone who already has a website connected to Google Search Console: bloggers, creators with a portfolio site, freelancers with client sites, and small business owners with a company website. If you have no Search Console property yet, this tool has nothing to read, so setting up Search Console for your site comes first. It suits people who want decisions based on their own traffic, not on generic advice.

How it fits the Claude Code workflow. Search Console MCP is the data layer of the workflow. Everything else in this guide depends on knowing what is already happening on your site. A practical way to use it is to start every SEO session with your own data: ask which queries are close to ranking, which pages need better titles, and which topics already bring traffic. Then use those answers to decide what to write, what to fix, and what to track. Because the answers come from your own Search Console data, the next steps are grounded in reality instead of guesswork.

Tool 2: Open SEO

What it does. Open SEO is an open source SEO alternative that puts the everyday SEO toolkit in one place: keyword research, rank tracking, backlink analysis, and site audits. Where Search Console MCP tells you what is already happening on your site, Open SEO helps you plan what to do next: find keywords worth targeting, see where your pages rank, check who links to you, and audit your site for problems that hold rankings back.

Who it suits. Open SEO suits creators and freelancers who need the core SEO toolkit without paying for a large subscription. It is a natural fit for people running one or a few sites, for beginners learning SEO by doing it, and for anyone who prefers open source tools they can inspect and run themselves. Because it is an open source project, expect to follow its current setup documentation, and expect the experience to be more hands-on than a polished paid dashboard.

How it fits the Claude Code workflow. Open SEO is the research and tracking layer of the workflow. Use it after you have looked at your own Search Console data: research the keywords your data suggests, check your current rankings for them, and audit the pages you plan to improve. Then, when Claude Code helps you rewrite a title, restructure a page, or draft a new article, you can return to Open SEO to track whether the ranking moved. Research, action, and tracking stay in one loop instead of scattered across five tools.

Tool 3: Geo Optimizer

What it does. Geo Optimizer is about visibility in AI search. Search is no longer only a list of blue links: AI assistants now answer questions directly, and they cite the content they draw on. Geo Optimizer helps you understand how your website appears in AI search, and whether an AI assistant such as ChatGPT may cite your content when it answers a question in your niche.

Who it suits. Geo Optimizer suits creators and businesses whose future traffic may come from AI answers as much as from classic search results. If you publish guides, reviews, comparisons, or explainers, AI answers are already competing with your pages. It also suits anyone who wants to know, before writing more content, whether their existing pages are even visible to AI search in the first place.

How it fits the Claude Code workflow. Geo Optimizer is the visibility check at the end of the workflow. After you have used your Search Console data and Open SEO research to create or improve a page, use Geo Optimizer to see how that page stands in AI search. If an AI assistant would not cite it, the fix is usually about clarity: a direct answer near the top, clear facts, clean structure, and content that an AI can quote without guessing. Claude Code can help you rewrite the page in exactly that shape, and then you check again. Data, research, content, and AI visibility become one repeatable loop.

The Simple Weekly Workflow

You do not need a complicated system to use these three tools together. A simple weekly loop is enough:

  • Open your own data first. Use Search Console MCP to find one keyword opportunity and one page that needs a better title or introduction.
  • Research before you write. Use Open SEO to check the keyword, see where you rank, and audit the page you plan to change.
  • Fix or create one page. Let Claude Code help you rewrite the title, tighten the introduction, or draft the new section, using the research you just did.
  • Check AI visibility last. Use Geo Optimizer to see whether the improved page stands a chance of being cited in AI search, and adjust the clarity of the answer if it does not.
  • Track and repeat. Watch the ranking and the traffic for that page, then start the next loop with the next opportunity.

One page per week, improved with real data, beats ten pages written on guesswork. The tools are free, but the discipline is what produces the result.

The Honest Limits of Free SEO Tools

Free tools remove the subscription cost, not the work. Keep these limits in mind before trusting any tool blindly.

  • No tool guarantees a ranking. Rankings depend on competition, content quality, site authority, and time. A tool can show you the opportunity; it cannot promise the result.
  • Always verify important numbers in the source itself. If a tool reports a surprising traffic figure or ranking, check it in Search Console or the ranking report before you act on it.
  • Open source tools need more hands-on setup than paid dashboards. Read the project’s current documentation, and do not paste private data, passwords, or client information into any tool you have not checked.
  • AI visibility is a moving target. AI search changes quickly, and a page that is cited today may not be cited tomorrow. Treat Geo Optimizer results as a snapshot, and recheck pages that matter to you.
  • Free does not mean effortless. These three tools save money, but they still need your judgment: which keyword is worth chasing, which page deserves the rewrite, and which result is real.

Used with those limits in mind, the three tools cover the full loop: your own data, your research, and your visibility in both classic and AI search.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do these three tools replace Ahrefs and Semrush?

For many creators and small sites, they cover the everyday jobs: your own search data, keyword research, rank tracking, backlinks, audits, and AI visibility. Large teams with heavy competitive research needs may still want a paid platform. Start with the free tools, and only pay when you hit a specific job they cannot do.

Do I need Claude Code to use these tools?

Claude Code is what turns the three tools into one workflow, because it can work across your data, your research, and your content in a single session. The tools themselves serve their own purposes too, but the workflow in this guide is built around asking Claude Code questions and letting it act on the answers.

Is my Search Console data safe with an MCP connection?

An MCP connection reads the data you connect it to, so only connect it to properties you own, and only use tools you trust. Never connect a client’s property without their permission, and never paste passwords or private credentials into prompts. Your Search Console data is your business asset; treat access to it the same way.

What is GEO, and why should I care about AI search?

GEO stands for generative engine optimization: making your content visible and citable when AI assistants answer questions. As more people get answers directly from AI instead of clicking through search results, being cited in those answers becomes a new kind of traffic. Geo Optimizer exists to show you where you stand in that new landscape.

Start With Your Own Data, Not Another Subscription

The most expensive SEO mistake is paying for a big platform before you understand your own site. Start the other way around. Look at your own Search Console data, research one keyword properly, improve one page, and check whether AI search can see it. Repeat that loop every week, and your SEO stops being guesswork.
